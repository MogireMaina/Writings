## Slide 2: Core Claims (115 words)

**Claim 1: Transnational Legal Exposure**
Qatar shares control of the world's largest gas reserve with Iran, creating unique legal exposure under extraterritorial sanctions (OFAC). These sanctions override territorial sovereignty, reducing Qatar's control from substantive to nominal.

**Claim 2: Contradiction to State Sovereignty**
Economic interdependence at critical chokepoints fundamentally contradicts dominant legal doctrine of state sovereignty. Weaponised interdependence violates UNCLOS Articles 56, 77 and Qatar's Law No. (8) of 2004.

**Claim 3: Multi-Forum Legal Strategy Reveals Gaps**
Qatar's simultaneous litigation (ICJ, ICAO, CERD) during 2017-2021 blockade revealed major gaps in protecting chokepoint states from economic warfare.

## Slide 3: Research Methodology (119 words)

**1. Doctrinal Legal Analysis**
Examines sovereignty, jurisdiction, and treaty obligations (UNCLOS, Chicago Convention). Reveals critical gap: remedies remain procedural rather than substantive.

**2. Comparative Case Study**
Compares Qatar (2017-2021), Russia (2022-present), Iran (2011-2025), Taiwan (2022-present). Demonstrates international law distinguishes by legal form rather than economic effect—territorial blockades trigger aviation law, but functional blockades lack legal remedy.

**3. Interdisciplinary Integration (McCrudden Framework)**
Applies 'two-way traffic' between law and social sciences to understand why economic power overrides legal validity. Example: Russian oil price cap works through market control (95% insurance concentration), not legal authority.

## Slide 4: Key Findings & Legal Gaps (125 words)

**ICJ Jurisdictional Victory Without Substantive Protection**
ICJ affirmed ICAO jurisdiction (2020), but case settled without merits determination or compensation (2021). International law provides formal remedy but no practical protection—reactive, not preventative.

**Form vs. Effect Problem**
Current law distinguishes by legal form (blockade/sanctions/export controls) rather than economic effect. Territorial blockade triggers ICAO; insurance blockade and tech export controls with identical coercive impact have no forum. International law fails to recognize functional equivalence.

**Weaponised Interdependence & Erosion of Sovereignty**
Extraterritorial sanctions (OFAC) create artificial legal boundaries that override sovereign equality. Fishman's 'invisible infrastructure' (dollar clearing, SWIFT, maritime insurance) enables economic warfare without military force, revealing sovereignty is nominal when compliance infrastructures privatize enforcement.
