Introduction
•Categorisation a central topic in philosophy, psychology and 
linguistics
•Cognitive tool implicated in learning, planning, communication 
and economy of knowledge (Croft & Cruse 2004: 74)
3The act of categorisation is one of the most basic human cognitive 
activities. Categorisation involves the apprehension of some individual 
entity, some particular of experience, as an instance of something 
conceived more abstractly that also encompasses other actual and 
potential instantiations. (Croft & Curse 2004: 74)

The Classic View
•The ‘classical model’ traceable to Aristotle
•Categories defined by sets of necessary and 
sufficient features
•Necessary in so far as required of all 
members
•Sufficient in so far as enough to 
guarantee membership
•Found in formal and structuralist approaches 
to lexical semantics (Geeraerts 2010)

Prototypes
•Classical model debunked by experimental research of 
Eleanor Rosch (e.g. Rosch 1973, 1978; Rosch & Mervis  1975)
•Developed alternative prototype  model of category 
structure
•Goodness -Of-Exemplar  (GOE) experiments
•Subjects presented with a category and a list of putative 
members which asked to rate, on a 1 -7 scale, according to 
how good an example they are of the category
•Combining results across subjects shows graded structure 
organised around a prototype

Prototypes
•Levels of categorisation – more specific categories nested 
in more inclusive ones
•E.g. fruit > apple  > granny smith
•Basic level categories defined by substantive 
characteristics:
•Level at which patterns of behavioural interaction most 
identifiable
•Level at which clear visual image can be formed
•Level at which part -whole information represented
•Level used for everyday reference
•Level to which subordinate items most rapidly categorised 
as belonging

Theory of Radial Categories
•Model developed in CL as explicit 
theory of knowledge 
representation accounting for 
prototype effects (Lakoff 1987; Taylor 
2003)
•Categories are conceptual 
structures in which a range of 
lexicalised concepts are organised 
radially in relation to a central 
prototype
•Gradient structure and fuzzy 
boundaries

•Relations between words and

Theory of Radial Categories
•Category structure and prototype effects born of idealised cognitive 
models (cf. frames) – conceptual structures by means of which we 
organise our knowledge (Lakoff 1987: 68)
•Abstractions across experience and thus degrees of ‘fit’ with potential 
instantiations
•Prototypicality arises where more or less perfect fit between ICM and 
specific case
•E.g. BACHELOR  < MARRIAGE
•The Pope / Tarzan

Theory of Radial Categories
•In many cases, prototypicality dependent on combinations of ICMs –   
cluster models (Lakoff 1987: 74 -76)
•E.g. MOTHER
•BIRTH  MODEL  / GENETIC  MODEL  / NURTURANCE  MODEL  / MARITAL  MODEL  / 
GENEALOGICAL  MODEL
•Stepmothers, surrogate mothers, adoptive mothers, biological 
mothers etc. all mothers but less prototypical by virtue of deviating 
from one or more ICM

The ‘Dictionary’ View
•Linguistic meaning, as represented in the 
lexicon, is distinct from world knowledge
•Semantics is concerned with ‘core’ 
linguistic meaning only - denotation
•Connotation  not included
•The lexicon is characterised as a kind of 
mental dictionary 
•Entries defined in terms of semantic 
features or components

Frames
•Definition possible only with 
reference to specific context – 
frame
•Structured bundles of knowledge 
representing real -world situations, 
arrangements, scenarios etc. 
•Encyclopaedic
•Inclusive of function, affect etc.
•Open -ended but accessed with 
different degrees of salience
21The particular organisation of knowledge which stands 
as a prerequisite to our ability to understand the meaning 
of … associated words  (Fillmore 1985: 224)
Any system of concepts related in such a way that to 
understand any one of them you have to understand the 
whole structure in which it fits (Fillmore 1982: 111)

Frames
•COMMERCIAL  EVENT  frame
•Words buy, sell, pay, spend, cost, charge, 
tender, change and discount  meaningful 
only within COMMERCIAL  EVENT  frame
•Minimally made up of four core elements : 
BUYER , SELLER , MONEY , GOODS
•Words foreground roles and relations within 
frame and encode perspective
•Accounts for argument structure ( valence )

Frames and Categories
•Frames disambiguate polysemous words
•E.g. menu
•Meaning dependent on currently active frame
•Frames represent prototypical scenarios
•E.g. MARRIAGE  CEREMONY  or RESTAURANT
•Prototypes provide default assignments for frame elements
•Not fixed but dependent on currently active frame
•E.g. ball or dog
