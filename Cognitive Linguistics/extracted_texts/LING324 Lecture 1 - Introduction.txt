•Developed originally in the 1980s in opposition to dominant approaches of the time:
•Formal and truth -conditional semantics (e.g. Montague 1974)
•Generative transformational grammar (e.g. Chomsky 1957, 1965)
5[A]n approach to language study based on the assumptions that our linguistic 
abilities are firmly rooted in our general cognitive abilities, that meaning is 
essentially conceptualisation, and that grammar is shaped by usage. (Dabrowska 
& Divjak 2015: 1)

What is Cognitive Linguistics?
•Instead, Cognitive Linguistics:
•Rejects the idea of an autonomous language 
module
•Presents a unified account of grammar, 
semantics and pragmatics
•Considers all elements of language to be

•Offers a naturally intuitive and psychologically 
plausible model of linguistic description
•Considers linguistic structure to emerge through 
usage
•Is functionalist and relativist in orientation

What is Cognitive Linguistics?
•In illustration of the semantic properties of grammatical constructions, consider the double -object 
or ditransitive construction vs. the prepositional indirect -object constuction  (the dative alternation ):
1.(a) Ivy gave Wilbur a book
       (b) Ivy gave a book to Wilbur 
2.(a) Ivy gave the fence a coat of paint
       (b) ?Ivy gave a coat of paint to the fence
3.(a) ?Wilbur sent America a parcel
       (b) Wilbur sent a parcel to America
•Ditransitive construction focusses on the result  of the process while prepositional construction 
foregrounds movement  within it (Langacker 1990: 13)
•2(b) odd because coat of paint not normally conceived as moveable object while 3(a) odd because 
America does not come into possession of the parcel as a result of the process
•No deep structure from which synonymous forms derived nor one derived from the other but 
alternate constructions which exist because meaningfully distinct

What is Cognitive Linguistics?
11The standard transformational analysis of these sentences treats them as 
synonymous and derives them from a common deep structure: depending on the 
particular choice of deep structure, to is either deleted or inserted transformationally , 
and the nonsubject  nominals are permuted in the course of deriving the surface form 
of either (a) or (b). Cognitive grammar does not posit abstract deep structures, and 
neither sentence type is derived from the other – they are claimed instead to 
represent alternate construals of the profiled event. [1](a) and (b) differ in meaning 
because they employ subtly different images to structure the same conceived 
situation. (Langacker 1986: 14)

What is Cognitive Linguistics?
•Returns to the Saussurian notion of the linguistic sign 
which relates linguistic forms to concepts (meanings)
•Radical claim of CL is that this extends to units both 
smaller and larger than lexical units (words), including 
phonemes, morphemes and grammatical constructions
•CL also revitalises the linguistic relativity hypothesis as 
conventional ways of encoding specific situations differ 
across languages (see Pederson 2012)

Key Claims
•Claim 1: Language is NOT an autonomous cognitive module
•Language relies on domain -general cognitive processes
•The processes that support language are not unique to language but 
operate also in other areas of cognitive experience such as memory, 
perception, imagination and reason
•Structures and processes of language not propositional or algorithmic 
but conceptual  and imagistic
•Analogues in other areas of cognitive experience and can be described 
appropriately in same terms
•Cognitive commitment: “a commitment to make one’s account of 
human language accord with what is generally known about the mind 
and the brain, from other disciplines as well as our own” (Lakoff 1990: 40)

Key Claims
•Claim 4: Language encodes experience
•There is no such thing as synonymy
•Words may share the same denotation but differ in their 
connotation
•Principle of no synonymy: If two constructions are syntactically 
distinct, they must be semantically or pragmatically distinct 
(Goldberg 1995: 67)
•E.g. ditransitive versus prepositional structure
•Alternate linguistic formulations impose contrasting construals  on 
(evoke contrasting images of) the situations they describe
•(a) The car parked in the street
•(b) The car parked on the street

Key Claims
•Claim 5: Language is grounded in experience ( i)
•The meanings attached to linguistic expressions are 
rooted in our experience of the world, including 
experiences we have of our bodies
•Image schemas 
•Embodiment thesis: features of the mind, including 
language, are shaped by the kind of bodies we have 
and the experiences they afford of the physical 
environment (e.g. Gibbs 2005)
•Experiential realism: an objective world exists but our 
experience of it, to which language relates, is 
subjective, mediated by our bodies (Lakoff 1987)
