Words and Rules
•Two distinct systems:
•Lexicon - words
•Grammar  - symbol -manipulating rules governing inflection and 
combination of words
•Sentences built up from words
•Meaning of a sentence computed ad hoc to recover thought 
expressed
4
Inside everyone’s head there is a finite algorithm with the ability to generate an 
infinite number of potential sentences, each corresponding to a distinct thought. The 
meaning of a sentence is computed from the meanings of the individual words and 
the way they are arranged. (Pinker 1998: 220)

Symbolic Assemblies
•One system: continuity of grammar and lexicon
•Language consists of system of form -meaning 
pairings or symbolic assemblies
•Theoretical austerity where just three elements 
required:
•Semantic structures (conceptualisations)
•Phonological structures (sounds, gestures, orthographic 
representations)
•Symbolic structures (in which semantic and 
phonological structures conventionally linked)
5
Grammatical structures do not constitute an autonomous formal system or 
level of representation: they are claimed instead to be inherently symbolic, 
providing for the structuring and conventional symbolization of conceptual 
content. Lexicon, morphology, and syntax form a continuum of symbolic units, 
divided only arbitrarily into separate components. (Langacker 2002: 1)

Word Classes
•CG provides semantic characterisation of word 
classes 
•Universal and fundamental classes (e.g. nouns and verbs)
•Highly schematic
•Determined by profiling
•Nominal predications profile things  
•Include nouns
•Based on grouping
•A conceptually autonomous entity in any domain
•Relational predications profile relationships  
•Process  (verbs) vs. non-processual  relation (adjective, 
adverb, preposition)
11tWhat determines an expression’s grammatical category is 
not its overall conceptual content, but the nature of its 
profile in particular. (Langacker 2008: 98)
A nominal predication presupposes the interconnections 
among a set of conceived entities, and profiles the region thus 
established. On the other hand, a relational predication 
presupposes a set of entities, and profiles the interconnections 
among these entities. (Langacker 2002: 74 -75)

•Future: speaking time occurs prior to event 
•Interacts with perfective/imperfective verbs
•English perfective verbs resist simple present tense, 
except in specialised circumstances such as sports

Aspect
•Aspect construes processes as ongoing vs complete:
•Progressive : ongoing
•Non-progressive : complete
•Progressive aspect marked by be … -ing
•Interacts with perfective/imperfective distinction
•English perfective verbs accept progressive aspect while 
imperfective verbs do not
a) Wilbur is/was reading the story
b) *Wilbur is/was knowing the story  
•Progressive aspect converts perfective process into imperfective 
one by profiling internal portion that excludes endpoints (IS1)
•Present tense further delimits profile to portion coincident with 
speech event (IS2)

Clause Structure
•Grammar “reflects our basic experience of moving, perceiving, and acting 
on the world” (Langacker 2008: 4) 
•Conceptual archetypes – Schematic conceptions of fundamental aspects 
of experience (action, motion, perception, location)
•Associated with specific clause types
•Archetypal conceptions provide prototypical semantic values of clause structures
•Clause structures provide default coding for archetypal conceptions
•Archetypal roles : agent, patient, instrument, experiencer, mover, zero

Clause Structure
•Archetypal conception associated with transitive  
clause = type of action chain 
•Action chain = “our conception of objects moving 
through space and impacting one another through 
forceful interaction” (Langacker 2008: 355)
•Canonical event model – arguably most typical of all 
occurrences = two -participant action chain
