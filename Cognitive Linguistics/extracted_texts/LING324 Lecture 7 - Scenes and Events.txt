Figure/Ground
•Linguistic forms in discourse organise cognitive representation (CR) 
of target situation
•Most obvious way scene organised in CR is Figure/Ground
•Reflective of perceptual experience
•Based on objective properties
•E.g. size; fixity of location; geometric complexity; topicality;

Thus, much as certain linguistic forms in a portion of discourse 
organise a referent situation in terms of configurational structure, 
so other linguistic forms specify where one is to locate one’s

Configurational System
•Plexity
•Uniplex  = quantity consisting of only one element
•Multiplex  = quantity consisting of multiple elements
•Encoded through number (singular vs. plural) and lexical 
aspect (semelfactive vs. iterative)
•Boundedness
•Bounded  = quantity conceived as having boundary
•Unbounded  = quantity conceived as without boundary
•Encoded through count/mass and grammatical aspect 
(perfect vs. imperfect)
•Bounding  vs. portion excerpting
10MATTER ACTION
Bounded He drank a glass of champagne He left the party
Unbounded He drank champagne He is leaving the partyMATTER ACTION
Uniplex A bird flew  in He sighed ( once)
Multiplex Birds flew in He kept sighing

Force -Dynamic System
•Structures events conceived as forceful
•Constitutes naïve physics of conceptual system
•Generalises over causation  to also include relations of 
letting, helping and hindering 
•Lexical and grammatical representation
•Push, pull, stay, remain, block, stop, overpower
•Against  (preposition), because/despite (conjunction), keep  
(auxiliary)
•Extends metaphorically to social and psychological 
domains
•Characterises grammatical category of modal verbs
•Can, may, should, could, must …

Force -Dynamic System
•Accounts for deontic  and epistemic  senses of modal verbs (Talmy  2000; 
Sweetser 1990)
•Deontic: permission/obligation
51. The cake can/may/should/must stay in the box
•Epistemic: truth/certainty
52. The pear could/may/should/must be ripe by now
•Epistemic senses derived metaphorically from deontic
•Evidence and reason act as antagonist
•Must  = ‘the available evidence forces me to the conclusion that P’
•May = ‘I am not barred by any alternative evidence from drawing the conclusion that P’
