# Dissertation Proposal

## Title: The Evolution of US-GCC Relations: Trump's 2025 Gulf Visit as a Strategic Turning Point

---

## 1. Background and Context

President Donald Trump's visit to Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates from May 13-16, 2025, marked his first major foreign trip of his second presidential term. This visit carries profound symbolic and substantive significance for understanding the evolving dynamics of US-Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) relations in an era of global power transition and strategic realignment. Unlike his predecessors, Trump chose the Gulf region over traditional European allies or neighbouring countries for his inaugural overseas visit, signalling a deliberate shift in American foreign policy priorities (TRENDS Research and Advisory, 2025).

The visit came at a critical juncture in international relations, characterised by intensifying US-China competition, the ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict, nuclear negotiations with Iran, and the humanitarian crisis in Gaza. Moreover, the international economic context—marked by record protectionism, sovereign debt crises, persistent inflation, and expanding military expenditures—heightened the strategic importance of the Gulf states as economic partners and geopolitical stabilisers (TRENDS Research and Advisory, 2025). Trump's announcement that the United States would adopt the term "Arabian Gulf" instead of "Persian Gulf" further underscored the symbolic dimension of American support for these key regional partners.

The visit resulted in unprecedented economic commitments: Saudi Arabia pledged $600 billion in US investments, Qatar committed $1.2 trillion, and the UAE announced $1.4 trillion in investment plans over the next decade. These agreements spanned critical sectors including artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing, energy infrastructure, defence technology, and aviation (TRENDS Research and Advisory, 2025). Beyond economics, the visit addressed pressing security concerns, including defence deals worth hundreds of billions of dollars, collective security arrangements, and the Gulf states' emerging role as mediators in global conflicts.

This dissertation seeks to analyse Trump's 2025 Gulf visit as a concentrated moment that captures the multifaceted nature of contemporary US-GCC relations. Rather than examining a broad historical timeframe, this research treats the visit as a critical case study that reveals the political, economic, military, and diplomatic dynamics shaping strategic alignment between the United States and the Gulf monarchies.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

While existing scholarship has extensively documented the historical evolution of US-GCC relations, particularly following the Arab Spring (2010-2012) and perceived American disengagement from the Middle East, there is limited focused analysis of how specific high-level diplomatic events crystallise and reveal underlying strategic dynamics. Trump's 2025 Gulf visit provides a unique opportunity to examine how symbolic diplomacy, economic statecraft, and security guarantees converge to shape alliance politics in a multipolar world.

Three critical gaps emerge in the current literature. First, while studies have examined the GCC states' pursuit of strategic autonomy and hedging strategies vis-à-vis rising powers like China and Russia (Guzansky, 2015; Ulrichsen, 2020), less attention has been paid to how concentrated diplomatic moments—such as major state visits—function as vehicles for negotiating the terms of continued strategic partnership with the United States. Second, existing research often treats the GCC as a monolithic bloc, overlooking the divergent strategies and priorities of individual member states, particularly Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE, whose relations with the United States have evolved differently (Kamrava, 2016). Third, the intersection of economic diplomacy, defence cooperation, and regional security concerns within a single diplomatic event has not been adequately theorised.

Trump's visit raises fundamental questions about the nature of contemporary alliance politics: To what extent do massive investment pledges and defence deals represent genuine strategic alignment versus transactional bargaining? How do individual GCC states leverage high-profile diplomatic moments to advance their distinct national interests while maintaining collective regional influence? What does this visit reveal about the changing balance of power in the Gulf and the broader Middle East?

---

## 3. Research Questions

This dissertation is guided by three interrelated research questions:

**Primary Research Question:**
How does Trump's 2025 Gulf visit illuminate the strategic realignment of US-GCC relations, and what does it reveal about the evolving nature of alliance politics in an era of great power competition?

**Secondary Research Questions:**
1. How did Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE strategically utilise Trump's visit to advance their respective national interests in the political, economic, and military domains?
2. To what extent does the visit reflect a shift from traditional security-based partnerships to multidimensional strategic relationships encompassing technology, investment, and diplomatic mediation?
3. How do the outcomes of the visit—particularly the massive investment commitments and defence agreements—relate to the GCC states' broader pursuit of strategic autonomy and hedging strategies?

---

## 4. Research Objectives

The dissertation aims to:

1. **Analyse the political dimensions** of Trump's visit, including discussions on US-Iran nuclear negotiations, the Gaza war, the Abraham Accords, Syrian reintegration, and Ukraine mediation.

2. **Examine the economic dimensions**, focusing on the unprecedented investment commitments, sectoral priorities (AI, energy, manufacturing), trade policy implications, and developmental goals of the GCC states.

3. **Assess the military and security dimensions**, including defence deals, security guarantees, the China factor, and divergent threat perceptions among the three Gulf states.

4. **Conduct a comparative analysis** of how Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE approached the visit differently, revealing their distinct strategic priorities and levels of alignment with US interests.

5. **Contextualise the visit** within broader global power dynamics, including US-China competition, the international economic crisis, and the GCC states' emerging role as global mediators and investors.

6. **Contribute to theoretical debates** on strategic autonomy, hedging, and alliance politics in the Global South.

---

## 5. Theoretical Framework

This dissertation employs a multi-theoretical approach drawing on alliance theory, strategic autonomy, and hedging frameworks.

**Strategic Autonomy** refers to states' capacity to pursue independent foreign policies while maintaining crucial partnerships (Fiott, 2018). For the GCC states, strategic autonomy involves balancing traditional security dependence on the United States with diversified economic and diplomatic partnerships with China, Russia, and other emerging powers. Trump's visit provides insight into how Gulf states negotiate autonomy within continued alignment with Washington.

**Hedging Theory** explains how secondary powers manage uncertainty by cultivating multiple partnerships to avoid over-dependence on any single great power (Kuik, 2008). The GCC states have engaged in hedging behaviour since the perceived US withdrawal from the region under Obama. The massive investment commitments during Trump's visit may represent a recalibration of hedging strategies in response to renewed American engagement.

**Alliance Politics and Transactionalism** provide lenses for understanding the bargaining dynamics evident in the visit. The Trump administration's transactional approach to foreign policy—emphasising deals, investments, and reciprocal benefits—differs from traditional alliance frameworks based on shared values and long-term commitments (Brands, 2018). Analysing the visit through this lens reveals how contemporary alliance politics increasingly blend security, economics, and symbolic diplomacy.

---

## 6. Methodology

This research adopts a **qualitative, comparative case study design**, focusing on three GCC states: Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE. This approach allows for systematic examination of how different domestic contexts, strategic priorities, and threat perceptions shape engagement with the United States (Yin, 2018).

**Data collection** relies on documentary analysis of primary and secondary sources. Primary sources include official statements from the White House, the US State Department, and GCC foreign ministries; bilateral agreements and memoranda of understanding; defence cooperation frameworks; and speeches delivered during the visit. Secondary sources encompass scholarly literature on Gulf politics and US foreign policy, think tank reports from organisations such as the Carnegie Middle East Center and the Gulf Research Center, and analytical articles from TRENDS Research and Advisory and similar institutions (Bowen, 2009).

**Data analysis** employs thematic analysis to identify patterns, discourses, and strategic narratives across political, economic, and military dimensions (Braun and Clarke, 2006). Process tracing will establish causal connections between the visit's outcomes and broader trends in US-GCC relations, while comparative analysis will highlight divergences among the three case countries (Bennett and Checkel, 2015).

**Limitations** include restricted access to classified diplomatic communications and internal decision-making processes. These will be addressed through triangulation of multiple public sources and explicit acknowledgement of analytical boundaries.

---

## 7. Significance and Contribution

This dissertation makes several important contributions. **Empirically**, it provides comprehensive analysis of a landmark diplomatic event that has received limited scholarly attention despite its strategic significance. **Theoretically**, it advances understanding of how concentrated diplomatic moments function as sites of alliance renegotiation and strategic signalling in contemporary international relations. **Methodologically**, it demonstrates the value of focused case studies over broad historical analyses for capturing the complexity of alliance politics. **Practically**, the research offers insights for policymakers navigating the evolving US-GCC partnership and managing great power competition in the Gulf region.

---

## 8. Structure of the Dissertation

The dissertation comprises eight chapters. Chapter 1 introduces the research problem and objectives. Chapter 2 reviews relevant literature and establishes the theoretical framework. Chapter 3 details the methodology. Chapters 4, 5, and 6 analyse the political, economic, and military dimensions of the visit respectively. Chapter 7 provides critical analysis and examines broader implications, including intra-GCC dynamics, the impact of the Israel attack on Qatar, and global power competition. Chapter 8 concludes with findings, theoretical contributions, and policy implications.
